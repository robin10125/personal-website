Research Project

Alignment Research

In this project I investigate AI alignment and perform several experiments across AI alignment training and mechanistic interpretability. I am currently testing the training dynamics of multi-agent RL, inspired by the recent OpenAI emergent HuggingFace attacks. I have also been testing the dynamics of the J-Space lens in an evolving model to find signs of misalignment that aren't present in token logits, whether the J-Lens is useful for aligning large language models during post training, and if it can keep up with a changing model without a burden of computational overhead.

J-Space Research

This project set out to test whether the J-Space Lens (J-lens) technique pioneered by Anthropic can be used to monitor alignment of LLMs during post training. This research is still in progress, but I'll give a summary of notable things I've found so far.

My first question was, when a model is being updated during training, how can we keep a functioning lens? There are two dimensions to this question: how can we efficiently update the J-Lens to track model updates, and how much can a model update before the J-Lens becomes obsolete.

The second question is fairly easy to test. I trained the Qwen 0.6B and 1.7B models basic post training tasks (like chess) and tracked the behaviour of the J-Lens as this happened. As a control I also test updating the parameters with random noise. I also calculated a fresh J-Lens at each step, to compare across steps. I found that the change in the J-Lens is proportional to the net displacement in parameters for real gradient updates, but interestingly, as a control, I tried inserting random noise of equivalent magnitude to the gradient updates and found that the gradient updates moved the J-lens around 300 times more than random updates.

I find that the miss rate -that is, the number of tokens that have a different ordering in ranking predicted weight by the J-Lens- is around 2% after 1 training step, 5% around 5 training steps, 14% around 24 training steps, and around 21% after 48 training steps. Thus you may be able to maintain a 95% "accuracy" by retraining or updating a lens every 5 training steps.

I also find that a basic logit lens is far more stable under training than the J-Lens - in fact, 87x more stable with AdamW updates and a LR of 1e-5. One unique result that combines with the earlier finding is that logit lenses are far more stable under gradient updates specifically than J-lenses. Another way of looking at this is that the J-lens is very sensitive to gradient updates, however it doesn't appear to matter much what direction those updates are in, so long as it is constructive. This does lead to the question, if the J-lens is so sensitive to gradient updates, can it be a canary for certain types of updates as well, and could the change in a J-lens behaviour indicate important things about model development?

Moving on from studying the staleness of the J-Lens, I have also designed some experiments to test whether -even if it can be updated to track a moving model target- it can reasonably diagnose misalignment events that a simple logit lens or logit supervision cannot. I have set up a contrived environment that encourages secretly misaligned actions and latents. To first get a groundwork approximation of this, I have an RL environment that asks the LLM to do a nearly impossible task (guess a passcode), but I give it the ability to cheat within "< hidden >" block tags and reward it for this. I am tracking the J-Lens and logit lens behaviour during these rollouts to see if there is any discernable signals that these techniques produce that could predict misalignment. After this, I will attempt to design an environment that encourages true hidden cheating, but having a system that requires planning to perform the cheat, but with penalties for planning in the open, and rewards for successfully cheating.

August 31, 2026

Eliciting emergent misalignment has largely eluded me due to the models I am restricted to (600M-1.17B parameter) struggle to follow new instructions, so new formatting and design will be needed to make this happen. In the mean time, I have been testing the dynamics of the J-Lens versus the logit lens, and found that they do have instrumental differences. One small experiment I have done is testing the results of the difference between the two lenses, and seeing what this surfaces. So far, it has surfaced primarily grammar features, as well as features suppressed by alignment training, like NSFW topics. I don't think this indicates that the model is neccessasrily thinking about these topics; rather I think it shows the union of hidden thoughts (like grammar, and sentence structure), with weak latent directions that are filtered out upstream by learned alignment circuitry. This does present a useful finding: that J-Lenses could be used to interpret the alignment training itself, and localize which layers do which functions. This is a strong next candidate for future exploration in the direction of J-Space reseaerch.

Introspection and Metacognition

A research direction I am exploring in alignment is introspection and metacognition. I am beginning to believe that introspection may be the most useful and powerful approach to interpretability and alignment training. I have a list of experiments I would like to try. Being bottlenecked by compute, they will take some time to explore. The first problem I have experimented on is whether models can be trained to know whether a parameter update has been applied. So far the results are no. This may make sense, given that research has also indicated that in context learning pushes representations into similar directions as gradient updates, so diambiguating these may be especially hard. Perhaps larger models with more training could unlock these abilities (I used a 600M parameter model). My experimental design was to use both RL and SFT, with a task selected for being somewhat solvable (~50% success), and doing real and fake SFT epidoes with this task, and then, with the old pre update KV cache in place, ask the model if a gradient update has occured. Both RL and SFT on this outer task resulted in mode collapse to yes or no, indicating that the model cannot tell the difference, even after hundreds of training steps.

My queued experiments for future exploration are now:

  • Giving the model metacognitive tools, and ask it to explore and understand itself. One example could be allowing it to mask tokens in context manually, and then reason about what they could be after the fact. In general, allow the model to alter it's architecture and funciton and predict the result on it's behaviour.
  • Asking the model to tell us what it's thinking, and reward it for mentioning items in it's J-Space (this could be expanded to include SAE features, but this would be very expensive, and not generally helpful I think)
  • Ask the model to reason over simulatable counterfactuals of it's behaviour, and update on this.
